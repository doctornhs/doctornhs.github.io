  • QHow many years does a joint replacement last?Knee
    A

    In a study pooling joint registry data from the UK, Australia and other countries, about 82% of total knee replacements and about 70% of partial knee replacements lasted 25 years without another operation. Managing your weight, avoiding high-impact activities such as running and jumping, and having regular check-ups help them last longer.

  • QWhen can I walk after hip replacement?Hip
    A

    Most people begin practicing standing with a walker the day after surgery. Walking distance is increased gradually with pain and condition, and for about three months after discharge you avoid positions that can cause dislocation, such as crossing your legs or squatting. The exact schedule depends on the operation and your condition.

  • QMy knee popped and is swollen. Is it a meniscus tear?Knee
    A

    It may be. Especially in middle age, pain that starts with a pop on the stairs or when squatting raises suspicion of a meniscus root tear. Left untreated, a root tear can speed up arthritis, so it is worth checking with an MRI. Not every meniscus tear needs surgery, though; treatment depends on the tear pattern and location, your age and your symptoms.

  • QDo I have to remove the metal plate in my body?Fractures & Bone Health
    A

    In most cases, no. Removal is considered when the plate catches or hurts under the skin, when there is infection, or in growing children and young, active people. Removal surgery has its own risks, such as nerve injury or the bone breaking again, so the decision weighs discomfort against risk.

  • QI can feel a lump. Could it be cancer?Lumps & Tumors
    A

    Most lumps in the body are benign. A lump larger than 5 cm, deep, growing quickly or painful needs a thorough work-up. A lump that tingles when tapped may be a nerve tumor, so do not squeeze or remove it yourself; have it checked.

  • QHow can I tell a calf strain from an Achilles tendon rupture?Foot & Ankle
    A

    When the Achilles tendon ruptures, it often feels as if someone kicked you just above the heel or you hear a pop, and you usually cannot stand on your toes. Because other muscles still allow some walking, it is easily mistaken for a strain, so see a doctor right away if you have these symptoms. If treatment is delayed, the tendon ends retract and repair can become difficult.

  • QMy child broke a collarbone. Does it need surgery?Fractures & Bone Health
    A

    Most heal well without surgery. A child's bone has a thick periosteum and a strong ability to remodel, so it recovers well even when somewhat displaced. The lump (callus) felt during healing is normal and mostly flattens as the child grows. Surgery is considered when bone has broken through the skin or nerve or blood vessel injury is suspected.

    Related: How should a child's clavicle fracture be treated?

  • QI was told I have osteopenia. Do I need medication?Fractures & Bone Health
    A

    Osteopenia is the stage before osteoporosis, and not everyone needs medication. Whether to treat is decided by assessing future fracture risk from age, previous fractures, family history and other factors. Strength exercise and walking, calcium and vitamin D, not smoking, limiting alcohol and preventing falls help everyone.

  • QWhen should hallux valgus (bunion) be operated on?Foot & Ankle
    A

    Surgery is not done for the shape of the big toe alone. It is considered when pain continues despite conservative treatment such as wider shoes and orthoses, or when wearing shoes and walking become difficult. The operation depends on the degree of deformity and the condition of the joint.
