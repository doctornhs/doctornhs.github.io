Featured research

Level of evidence 1 · Randomized controlled trial · First author

If you explain “realistic expectations” before surgery, does satisfaction after joint replacement rise?

172 patients about to have joint replacement were randomly divided into two groups. One group received the usual education only; the other received additional education that explained the recovery course and realistic expectations in detail. One year after surgery, 83% were satisfied, compared with 65% in the group that received only the usual education. The difference was larger in people with a constitution especially sensitive to pain (central sensitization).

I also did a follow-up randomized study (2025) tracking the same patients to two years. The difference in satisfaction at one year had disappeared at two years. The effect of less pain and stiffness appeared from 6 months, and the effect on pain lasted to one year. It means that explanation before surgery helps people get through the hardest first year of recovery.

That is why I take time to explain before surgery. The first study was published in KSSTA (2023), the official journal of the European Society of Sports Traumatology, Knee Surgery and Arthroscopy (ESSKA), and the follow-up in the same journal (2025).

Analysis of 863 patients · First author

Do iron injections after joint replacement help?

I compared 863 patients who had joint replacement, between those who received an intravenous iron injection afterward and those who did not. In the iron group, hemoglobin fell less and recovered better at 2 and 6 weeks after surgery, and among those who had both knees done in separate operations, the hospital stay was also shorter. However, there was no difference in transfusion rate, complications or final functional outcome.

Only by knowing both the effect and the limits can I recommend it to the people who truly need it.

Artificial intelligence · 3 first-author articles

Reading the outcome in advance from a single image and preoperative information

I built a deep learning model that predicts whole-leg alignment from an ordinary knee X-ray alone, and it showed accuracy similar to values measured directly on a full-leg film. I also developed a model that predicts satisfaction after joint replacement from preoperative information, and a model that picks out cases in bowed-leg correction that need a two-level osteotomy.

I think of artificial intelligence not as a tool that replaces clinical care but as a tool that double-checks information easily missed in the same image.

Bone and soft tissue tumors: 4 articles

The rarer the tumor, the more important the record

At the Korea Cancer Center Hospital I analyzed the treatment results of extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma and myxoid liposarcoma, and the survival and complications of patients with a malignant bone tumor whose entire femur was replaced with a prosthesis. For tenosynovial giant cell tumor of the knee, I summarized how effective arthroscopic excision is depending on the extent of spread.
