A 22-year-old's collarbone broken playing soccer: why I added a mini-plate to the main plate

Hee Seung Nam, MD, Orthopedic Surgeon · November 23, 2026

This 22-year-old man fell while playing soccer and hurt his right shoulder. He came in after being told elsewhere that his collarbone was broken. He had no chronic conditions such as high blood pressure or diabetes. On examination, the broken end of the collarbone was pushing up against the skin from inside, so the skin was puckered inward (skin dimpling). The X-ray showed a midshaft clavicle fracture.

We also took a CT to see the exact shape of the fracture. The 3D images showed that the collarbone had broken straight across and the two pieces were displaced.

Does every clavicle fracture need surgery?

No. Many clavicle fractures heal with rest in a sling. Surgery is recommended in the following cases.

  • Large displacement: if the pieces are separated or shifted by more than the width of the bone, they may not heal well.
  • Shortening: if the pieces overlap and heal short, the shape and strength of the shoulder can be affected.
  • Skin pushed up: when the bone end presses on the skin from inside, the skin can be damaged or broken.
  • Young, active people: who need the bone to heal quickly and reliably to get back to daily life and sports.

A Canadian multicenter trial comparing plate fixation with sling treatment for displaced midshaft clavicle fractures also found fewer cases of non-healing and faster functional recovery after surgery. This patient's fracture was badly displaced and shortened, the skin was being pulled in, and he is a young man who loves sports, so we decided to operate.

Surgery: a superior main plate plus an anterior mini-plate

After putting the two broken pieces back in place, I fixed a main plate on top of the collarbone with screws. I then added a 2.0 mm mini-plate on the front of the collarbone.

In a transverse fracture the broken surfaces are flat, so they do not lock into each other well. A single superior plate can hold it, but when the arm is used, the fracture site is pushed back and forth. Adding a small plate on the front holds the bone from two directions, top and front, which makes the fixation much stronger. A biomechanical study has in fact shown that adding an anterior mini-plate to a standard superior plate makes the construct stiffer. Mini-plates are thin, so they barely stick out under the skin.

How I fix clavicle fractures by pattern

Clavicle fractures break in different shapes. I use a superior main plate as the base and choose how to reinforce it based on the fracture pattern.

PatternReinforcementWhy
TransverseAnterior 2.0 mm mini-plateFlat fracture surfaces interlock poorly, so the bone is held from two directions
Comminuted
(several pieces)		1.5 mm mini-plate, with Ethibond sutures if neededSmall fragments are fixed together first, and loose ones are tied down with strong suture
Simple spiral
(long and oblique)		2.0 mm lag screwThe long fracture surface lets one screw compress the two pieces together

Ethibond is a thick, strong suture that does not dissolve. It is excellent for wrapping and tying small bone fragments, and it is honestly one of my favorite materials.

Recovery after surgery

It varies with the patient and the fracture pattern, but it usually goes like this.

TimeWhat happens
Surgery to 2 weeksSling as needed for comfort. Gentle shoulder movement within pain limits. Wound check and stitch removal
2 to 6 weeksGradually increase shoulder range of motion. Avoid heavy lifting and leaning on the arm
6 weeks to 3 monthsStart strengthening while checking healing on X-ray
Around 3 monthsGradual return to contact sports such as soccer once healing is confirmed

The plates can stay in after the bone heals if they cause no trouble. If they can be felt under the skin or hurt when bumped during sports, they can be removed once the bone is solid enough.

Final thoughts

  • Not every clavicle fracture needs surgery. It is considered when the fracture is badly displaced, shortened or pushing on the skin.
  • A transverse clavicle fracture is held more firmly when an anterior mini-plate is added to the superior plate, gripping it from two directions.
  • For other patterns, such as comminuted or spiral fractures, I reinforce with mini-plates, sutures or lag screws.
  • In young, active patients, secure fixation lets rehabilitation and return to sports go as planned.

I look forward to the day he is back on the field kicking the ball freely.

This article is general medical information. Treatment and recovery vary with the fracture pattern, the degree of displacement, age and activity level, so please confirm with your doctor.
