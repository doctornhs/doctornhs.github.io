An elbow broken in a fall while carrying laundry: olecranon fracture and plate fixation

Hee Seung Nam, MD, Orthopedic Surgeon · November 12, 2026

A patient came in after tripping over the veranda threshold while carrying laundry out of the laundry room. The pain in her right elbow did not settle after the fall, and the next day she was admitted through the emergency room. It is a common kind of accident at home.

X-ray: the bone at the back of the elbow broke and pulled apart

The pointed bone you can feel at the back of a bent elbow is the olecranon. It is the upper end of the ulna, the inner bone of the forearm, and it hooks around the upper arm bone to form the hinge of the elbow joint. It often breaks when the elbow strikes the ground directly in a fall. The side X-ray showed this bone broken with the fragment pulled apart.

Why does an olecranon fracture pull apart?

The triceps, the muscle that straightens the elbow, attaches to the olecranon. When the olecranon breaks, the triceps pulls the loose fragment upward and the fracture gaps open. This causes two problems.

  • The force to straighten the elbow is not transmitted. The pull of the triceps no longer reaches the forearm, so it is hard to straighten the arm on your own.
  • The joint surface is out of line. The olecranon is part of the elbow joint surface, so if it heals out of place the joint can stiffen or develop arthritis.

So a fracture that has barely separated is treated with a splint or cast, while a separated fracture is usually aligned and fixed with surgery. For low-demand elderly patients, however, satisfactory results have been reported with nonoperative treatment even of separated fractures, so age and activity level are considered as well.

Tie it with wire, or fix it with a plate?

MethodWhat the surgery isMainly used for
Wire fixationTwo pins are inserted and a wire is looped in a figure of eight to pull the fragment together (tension band wiring).Simple fractures that break cleanly across
Plate fixationA plate shaped to fit the olecranon is placed on the back of the bone and fixed with several screws.Fractures with several fragments or an oblique fracture line, or when firmer fixation is needed

In a British randomized trial comparing the two methods (67 patients), arm function scores at one year did not differ. Complications, however, were more common with wire fixation (63% vs 38%), mostly because the pins and wire caught under the skin and had to be removed in a second operation (50% vs 22%).

Surgery: fixed with an olecranon plate

After checking the fracture pattern and joint surface on CT, we decided to fix it with a locking plate shaped to fit the olecranon. The back of the elbow was opened, the separated fragment was put back in place and the joint surface was checked to be smooth, and then the plate was placed on the back of the olecranon and fixed with screws in several directions.

After surgery: move early so it does not stiffen

  • A splint only briefly: right after surgery the elbow is protected in a splint for a short time, and once the wound has settled, bending and straightening exercises begin early. The elbow stiffens easily if immobilized for long.
  • Heavy loads later: until the bone heals, avoid pushing yourself up with the arm or lifting heavy objects, because these put a strong pull of the triceps on the olecranon.
  • The plate may be felt: the olecranon lies just under the skin, so the plate may be felt or be uncomfortable when you lean on the elbow. If it is bothersome, it can be removed after the bone has fully healed (Do you have to remove the metal plate in your body?).

In closing

  • If the back of your elbow is swollen and painful after a fall and you cannot straighten your arm on your own, it may be an olecranon fracture.
  • Separated olecranon fractures are usually fixed surgically to restore the force that straightens the arm and the joint surface.
  • Wire fixation and plate fixation give similar functional results, and the choice follows the fracture pattern.
  • After surgery, moving the elbow early so it does not stiffen is important.

This article is general medical information. Treatment can differ with the fracture pattern and the patient's condition, so please confirm through a medical consultation.
