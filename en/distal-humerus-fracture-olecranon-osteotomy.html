Fracture

A distal humerus fracture into the elbow joint, repaired through an olecranon osteotomy

Hee Seung Nam, MD, Orthopedic Surgeon · November 5, 2026

A 43-year-old man came in with right elbow pain after a fall.

The X-ray showed an intra-articular fracture, with the fracture line running into the joint, and it was truly severe. Plain X-rays alone cannot show exactly how many pieces the bone has broken into or which way they have shifted, so we took a CT.

Diagnosis: distal humerus fracture, AO/OTA type C1

The CT confirmed a distal humerus fracture, a break at the lower end of the upper arm bone, classified as AO/OTA type C1.

In the AO/OTA classification, type C is a fracture that completely involves the joint surface: the bone end that forms the joint has broken away and is no longer connected to the bone above. Within it, C1 is the form in which the fractures of the joint surface and the area just above it (metaphysis) are relatively simple. Unless such a fracture is put back exactly in place, it directly affects elbow movement and function.

Surgery: cutting the olecranon to expose the joint surface

I put great care into this operation. The joint surface of the distal humerus is hidden behind the olecranon, the bone at the back of the elbow, so it cannot be seen well as it is. We therefore deliberately cut and turned back the olecranon, an olecranon osteotomy, to expose the joint surface. With this view, the bone was put back in place while looking directly at the joint surface (reduction) and fixed with metal plates (fixation). The cut olecranon was reattached with a screw and wire.

Rehabilitation matters as much as surgery

I believe the key to treating elbow fractures is proper reduction and fixation without damaging the joint surface. But surgery is not the end. The elbow is prone to contracture, stiffening that reduces movement, so rehabilitation is as important as the operation. The reason for fixing the bone firmly is, in the end, to allow the elbow to move early.

In closing

  • Fractures that reach the elbow joint surface are hard to judge on X-rays alone, so CT is used to check the number and direction of the fragments.
  • A type C fracture that completely breaks the joint surface must be aligned exactly and fixed firmly to preserve elbow function.
  • An olecranon osteotomy is sometimes done to see the hidden joint surface directly.
  • After surgery, steady rehabilitation is needed so that the joint does not stiffen.

This article is general medical information. Treatment can differ with the fracture pattern and the patient's condition, so please confirm through a medical consultation.
